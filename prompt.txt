# Core Features:
Include all the basic features that you think a Trading Journal should have.

Also include all of the following extra features:

1. The "Why" Trigger (Logic Capture):
Instead of a long essay, have a small text field or dropdown to record your entry reason.
Minimalist approach: Use 3-letter codes like SUP (Support), BRK (Breakout), or GAP (Gap Fill).
Purpose: If you can’t state a clear "Why," the journal proves you were gambling, not trading.

2. Rule Adherence Toggle (The Truth Check):
A simple Yes/No checkbox: "Did I stick to my predefined trading plan?"
Purpose: This is the most important metric. You can lose money and still be a great trader if you followed your rules. This feature separates the "process" from the "outcome."

3. Risk-to-Reward (R:R) Expected vs. Actual:
Two numeric fields: What you planned to make vs. what you actually made relative to your risk.
Purpose: If your planned R:R is 1:2 but your actual is 1:0.5, you are "cutting your winners too early"—a common mistake for Indian retail traders.

4. Psychological State (Emotions):
A set of single-word tags to select how you felt during the trade: Calm, Fearful, Greedy, Impulsive.
Purpose: Over time, you might see a pattern where 80% of your losses happen when you tag a trade as Impulsive.

5. The "Mistake" Category:
A dropdown menu of common errors: FOMO, Chasing, Over-leveraged, Early Exit.
Purpose: This turns a loss into a lesson. A journal is meant to identify patterns of failure so you can fix them.

6. Net P&L (Post-Charges):
A formula that automatically calculates Indian market costs (STT, GST, SEBI charges, Brokerage).
Purpose: In India, high-frequency trading can be "Green" on paper but "Red" after taxes. A true journal shows you the take-home pay.

7. Before & After Screenshots:
Space for two images: The chart at the moment of entry and the chart after the exit.
Purpose: Humans are visual. Seeing a "perfect setup" that failed vs. a "messy setup" that won is the fastest way to train your brain's pattern recognition.

8. The "Single Lesson" Reflection:
A 140-character text box for one final thought after the trade is closed.
Purpose: Forces you to distill the entire experience into one actionable takeaway for tomorrow.
